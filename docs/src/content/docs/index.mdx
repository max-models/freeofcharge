## What is included

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Equilibria" icon="open-book">
		The poloidal flux from G-EQDSK files or the Solov'ev solution: the field, `rho = sqrt(psi_n)`, flux shells with their volumes and aspect ratios, the boundary and the first wall as simgeometry polygons.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Beams" icon="rocket">
		Markers of fast atoms aimed with `tangential_injection`: Gaussian width and divergence, a halo, energy components.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Deposition" icon="star">
		Every marker a straight line attenuated with Suzuki's stopping cross sections (as in ASCOT5): birth profiles, sampled fast-ion births with their pitch, shine-through and where it hits the wall.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Slowing down" icon="document">
		Classical (Stix) slowing down on the birth surface: heating of electrons and ions, fast-ion density and pressure, and the beam-driven current with electron shielding.
	</Card>
	<Card title="CPU, GPU, MPI" icon="setting">
		Arrays on the cunumpy backend (NumPy or CuPy); with MPI every rank traces its own markers and the profiles are summed once.
	</Card>
	<Card title="xarray output" icon="list-format">
		Profiles convert to xarray datasets with units, ready for plasma-plots.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>

## Where it fits

freeofcharge is the last stage of the max-models chain of a neutral-beam injector, from the
ion source to the plasma:

1. **Ion source and extraction** - [picnic](https://github.com/max-models/picnic) (particle-in-cell)
   and [wellbalanced](https://github.com/max-models/wellbalanced) (global and fluid models).
2. **Neutralizer** - [plasmacoll](https://github.com/max-models/plasmacoll), the collisions of the
   beam with the gas; freeofcharge's markers are plasmacoll `ParticleArrays`.
3. **The plasma** - freeofcharge: deposition, shine-through, heating and current drive.

The model is deliberately fast: straight lines and local slowing down, without orbit width,
prompt losses, charge-exchange losses of fast ions or 3D effects. That is what a design scan
over beam energy, aiming and density needs. For orbits and losses, the fast-ion births
(positions, velocities and weights) can be the starting points of an orbit-following code.
