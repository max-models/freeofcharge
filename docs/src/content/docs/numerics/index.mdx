freeofcharge follows a neutral beam into a tokamak or stellarator plasma: where its atoms are
ionised, how much shines through onto the wall, and where the fast ions heat the plasma and
drive current. Nothing is solved for the plasma itself. The equilibrium and the profiles are
inputs, and every stage is a closed form or a one-dimensional integral per marker, so a design
scan of the injector can call it in seconds.

Each equation on these pages is declared on the code that implements it, as a method card, and
checked by the tests: nine closed forms against SymPy rules, two discretisations against their
order of convergence. The pages of the section list the cards module by module; this one shows
how they fit together.

## The pipeline

Markers are streams of atoms (position, velocity, weight in atoms per second), split between
MPI ranks. A run is `deposit`, then `slow_down` on the flux shells of the equilibrium.

1. **[Equilibrium](/freeofcharge/numerics/equilibrium/).** The axisymmetric field from the
   poloidal flux $\psi(R, Z)$ and $F(\psi) = R B_\phi$, the radius $\rho = \sqrt{\psi_n}$, the
   shells with their volumes, $q$ and trapped fraction; the Solov'ev solution for the tests.
   3D equilibria come through adapters ([GVEC](/freeofcharge/numerics/gvec-equilibrium/), DESC,
   PolarFEEC) with the same interface.
2. **[Plasma profiles](/freeofcharge/numerics/profiles/)** of $n_e$, $T_e$, $T_i$ and the ion
   densities on $\rho$, quasi-neutral, zero density outside the last closed surface.
3. **[Beam markers](/freeofcharge/numerics/beam/):** a mono-energetic beam with Gaussian width
   and divergence, or the markers of a beamline model.
4. **[Beam stopping](/freeofcharge/numerics/stopping/):** the effective cross section
   $\sigma_s$ of all ionising channels, from Suzuki's fit.
5. **[Deposition](/freeofcharge/numerics/deposition/):** every marker a straight line,
   attenuated by $n_e \sigma_s$:

<MethodCard name="freeofcharge.deposition.deposit" headingLevel={3} />

6. **[Slowing down](/freeofcharge/numerics/slowing-down/):** each fast ion slows down on its
   birth surface by Stix's drag on the electrons and the ions, its pitch relaxed by pitch-angle
   scattering; the moments of the trajectories give the heating, the fast-ion density and
   pressure, and the current, shielded by the electrons:

<MethodCard name="freeofcharge.slowing_down.slow_down" headingLevel={3} />

7. **[Orbit-averaged pitch](/freeofcharge/numerics/trapping/):** at zero orbit width, trapped
   ions carry no current and passing ones their transit-averaged pitch; the scattering averaged
   over the orbit decays in modes, found by finite volumes in $\lambda$.

The **[injector chain](/freeofcharge/numerics/chain/)** adds closed forms for the stages before
the plasma (the source, the extraction, the neutralizer and the duct), for an end-to-end scan
of an injector.

## Units and conventions

SI units, except temperatures and particle energies in eV and Suzuki's fit, which takes the
beam energy in keV/amu. The beam is a hydrogen isotope (mass number 1 to 3), and the fast-ion
charge is $Z_b = 1$. $(R, \phi, Z)$ is right-handed; G-EQDSK files are read as they are, with
their COCOS signs.

## Not in the model

- No Grad-Shafranov solve: the equilibrium is given.
- No fast-ion distribution function: only its moments along the slowing-down trajectories.
- Zero orbit width: the ions stay on their birth surface; no guiding-centre orbits, no prompt
  or orbit losses, no wall loads from fast ions.
- No charge-exchange losses, no radial transport, no anomalous diffusion of the fast ions.
- No energy diffusion: the energy loss is the mean drag only.
- Stix's drag assumes $v_{Ti} \ll v \ll v_{Te}$; the ions join the thermal ions when they
  reach the end energy.
- Deposition only inside the last closed surface; the wall crossing is resolved to one step.
- No first wall for 3D equilibria.
- The chain's source has no model of the negative-ion surface production and uses illustrative
  rates.

## Verification

Besides the checks of the cards, the tests compare Suzuki's fit with ASCOT5's to $10^{-10}$, the
attenuation of a pencil beam with $e^{-n\sigma L}$, the slowing-down integrals with the
equations of motion, the pitch modes with a Monte Carlo of Lorentz scattering, and the
3D adapters with GVEC and DESC; the [ASCOT5 benchmark](/freeofcharge/benchmarks/ascot5/)
compares a whole run.
