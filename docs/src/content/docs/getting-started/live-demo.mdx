The beam is deposited along straight lines with Suzuki's stopping cross sections (`deposit`), and
each fast ion slows down on its flux shell (`slow_down`). Lower the beam energy or raise the
density and the beam stops near the edge; raise the energy or lower the density and more of it
shines through to the wall. Fast ions above the critical energy heat mainly the electrons, those
below it mainly the ions: the critical energy grows with the electron temperature, so a hotter
plasma or a slower beam moves the heating to the ions. A hotter plasma also drives more
current, since the fast ions take longer to slow down. Moving the tangency radius out towards
the magnetic axis makes the beam more parallel to the field and drives more current, until
beyond the axis the path through the plasma gets short and more of the beam shines through.

The same in a script:

```python
import freeofcharge as foc

equilibrium = foc.Equilibrium.solovev(6.2, 1.8, 1.7, 5.3)
plasma = foc.Plasma.parabolic(1e20, 2e4, ions={"D": (2, 1, 0.96), "He": (4, 2, 0.02)})
origin, direction = foc.tangential_injection(5.3, 12.0)
beam = foc.NeutralBeam.gaussian(
    origin, direction, 1e6, 16.5e6, width=0.3, divergence=5e-3, markers=1000, seed=1
)
deposition = foc.deposit(beam, equilibrium, plasma, step=0.02, births_per_marker=2, seed=2)
steady = foc.slow_down(deposition, plasma, equilibrium.flux_surfaces(deposition.rho_edges))
```
